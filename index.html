The world it lives in

In early childhood education, staffing isn’t back-office — it’s the law.

Aroha Consulting places vetted relief teachers into early-childhood centres across Auckland. When a teacher calls in sick, a centre can drop below its legally-mandated teacher-to-child ratio. An unfilled shift isn’t an inconvenience — it puts a centre out of compliance and tamariki at risk. Speed and trust are the product.

“Every morning, centres phoned Aroha with relief requests. A person matched teachers to shifts by hand, checked who was vetted and available, and rang around until each shift was filled.”

— the process Aroha Connect was built to replace.

The journey

Three releases, one direction of travel.

Each phase solved the problem in front of it — and, in hindsight, each one quietly laid the track for the next.

01
Phase 1 · 2023

Replace the phone.

Turn a fully manual scheduling process into a booking, scheduling and document-management app — with an admin panel to bulk-load relievers, define regions, and set pay rates. Post a shift, notify a reliever, confirm on accept.
Outcome — the phone stops being the system
02
Phase 2 · 2024

Take out the friction.

A unified reliever dashboard, in-app chat, automated timesheets, invoicing, automatic job distribution, geographic ring-fencing (“In My City”), reliever grading, and far richer admin tooling. The everyday experience gets fast for all three personas.
Outcome — the category tool for ECE relief
03
Phase 3 · Aroha Connect 3.0 · 2026

Tool becomes platform.

Multi-tenant SaaS, white-label client portals, a public REST API and an MCP layer — and the first deliberate step into agentic placement. The internal tool becomes a product other agencies can buy.
Outcome — a commercialisable, agent-ready platform
The challenge · 3.0

Three pressures arrived at once.

By 2026, the thing that made Aroha Connect successful was also what capped it. Growth, commercial ambition, and a shifting market all pushed on the same walls.

Pressure 01

The admin ceiling

Every new centre added manual load onto Aroha staff. Growth was throttled by human hours, not by demand.

Pressure 02

The single-agency ceiling

The platform was built for one agency — Aroha. Commercialising meant serving many, in isolation, each with their own branding and data.

Pressure 03

The relevance risk

Buyers are beginning to search, compare and book through AI agents. A platform that only speaks to humans through screens risks being routed around.

The question that framed 3.0
How do you commercialise the platform and make it ready for an agent-driven world — without abandoning the roadmap and momentum you already have?
Discovery & insight
The app was never the moat.

It’s tempting to assume the product is the interface. It isn’t. The defensible asset is the supply network of vetted relievers, the matching logic, and — above all — the compliance and credentialing data behind every placement.

Police vetting. Teacher registration. RS7. Teaching Council standards. Aroha already holds a current, structured record of exactly who is qualified, vetted and available.

Vetted supply Matching logic Police vetting Registration RS7 Council standards
In an agent-driven world, a machine-readable record of who is vetted, qualified and available right now becomes more valuable, not less. An agent can’t place a teacher it can’t verify — and Aroha already holds the verification.

The reframe: agent-readiness stopped being a threat to defend against and became an asset Aroha was uniquely positioned to expose. The strategy flipped from defensive to offensive.

Prioritisation & decisions

The calls — and what each one cost.

Agent-readiness is additive, not a pivot. The 3.0 commercialisation roadmap stayed intact; the API and MCP capability was layered on top of existing momentum rather than replacing it.

Decision 01

One capability layer, many consumers

The internal Aroha agent is built as the first client of the same public API + MCP layer sold to external clients — not as a separate engine.
Cost
More abstraction to design up front.
Payoff
No duplicate architecture, and the platform thesis is proven internally before it’s ever sold.
Decision 02

Crawl, walk, run on autonomy

Ship “agent suggests, human approves” (L1) first; graduate to a fully autonomous loop (L3) only once the ranking model has earned trust.
Cost
Slower to full automation.
Payoff
Trust is built on evidence, and early failures stay cheap and contained.
Decision 03

Trigger-agnostic by design

The agent fires on any vacancy signal. When “a permanent teacher declares leave” turned out to have no data model behind it, that became a deferral — not a blocker.
Cost
One shiny trigger deferred.
Payoff
A scope landmine avoided, and a foundation built on data that actually exists.
Decision 04

Deliberate deferrals

ChatGPT MCP moves to 3.1. Permanent-teacher leave tracking moves to later. Not everything valuable belongs in this release.
Cost
Fewer headline features at launch.
Payoff
Scope discipline keeps 3.0 shippable and coherent.
Decisions log Every open question was catalogued and every resolution recorded in a running log inside the PRD — the mechanism that kept scope from drifting across six feature areas.
What shipped · 3.0

A commercial platform, in six coordinated parts.

3.0 lands as six feature areas that turn the existing app into something other agencies can license — each scoped, wireframed, and tracked against a structured feature code.

AC3-001

MYOB integration

Approved timesheet hours pushed asynchronously to MYOB Business Pro.

AC3-010

SMS-to-jobSMS

Structured SMS shift requests via a printed reference card — no NLP, no ambiguity.

AC3-020

Client Admin portal

White-label, desktop-first. Centre Admins post shifts and approve timesheets themselves.

AC3-030

SuperAdmin

Replaces the old Admin Panel; impersonation is always flagged by an orange banner.

AC3-040

Public REST API

Versioned, Bearer-token auth, scoped per client. The platform’s front door.

AC3-050

MCP serverMCP

Claude and Microsoft CoPilot at launch; pilot centres first.

AC3-060

Pool forking

Centres run private pools of self-vetted casual staff alongside Aroha’s vetted network — ECE-first, built to expand.

One visual system ties it together — navy for Aroha, purple for private-pool context, orange for SuperAdmin impersonation — and origin badges (App · SMS · MCP) everywhere a shift can enter the system.

On the horizon · AC3-070

The shift that fills itself.

Around 70% of the machinery already exists — broadcast and direct offers, an auto-accept path, atomic accept/decline, escalation timers, an eligibility filter, favourites-first logic. Only two pieces are genuinely new.

AC3-071 · net-new

Candidate ranking model

Scores eligible relievers on booking history, reliability, proximity and preferences — beyond a simple filter.

Net-new

Offer orchestration layer

Runs sequential offers with timeouts and fallback logic — autonomously working down the ranked list.

Reflection

What the release really taught.

The teams that panic at “AI” rebuild and lose their moat. The ones that expose it win.

The moat was compliance, supply and matching data — the exact things an agent most needs.

Trust is a product decision. The autonomy ladder made AI placement shippable by making it accountable first.
